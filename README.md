# 🤝 Saath: how to listen when someone needs you

**साथ · सोबत · Together**

Most people don't need advice. They need someone who listens.
**Saath** is a free, simple guide that teaches anyone how to be that person for a friend, a family member, a classmate or a coworker.

## 1. Why listening matters

Many people feel frustrated, lonely or stuck simply because **nobody really listens to them**. Friends jump in with advice, change the subject, or say "it's not a big deal."

Being heard properly can make someone feel calmer, less alone, and more able to solve their own problem. You don't need any training to do this. You just need a few simple habits.

---

## 2. The 5 rules of good listening

1. **Give full attention.** Put your phone away. Look at them. Let them finish.
2. **Don't rush to fix it.** Most of the time, they want to be understood, not given a solution.
3. **Don't judge.** Their feelings are real to them, even if you would feel differently.
4. **Repeat back what you heard.** "So you're feeling left out at work?" This shows you're really listening.
5. **Keep it private.** Never share what they told you unless their safety is at risk.

---

## 3. What to say, and what not to say

| ✅ Try saying | ❌ Avoid saying |
|---|---|
| "That sounds really hard." | "Don't think like that." |
| "I'm here. Take your time." | "Others have it worse." |
| "Do you want advice, or do you just want me to listen?" | "You should just…" |
| "Thank you for telling me." | "It's not a big deal." |
| "How can I help right now?" | "Just be positive!" |
| "I can't fully understand, but I want to." | "I know exactly how you feel." |

**Tip:** silence is okay. You don't have to fill every pause.

## 4. When to suggest professional help

Listening helps a lot, but some situations need a trained person. Gently suggest a counsellor, doctor or helpline if your friend:

- talks about hurting themselves or not wanting to live
- has felt low for more than two weeks
- can't sleep, eat, study or work normally
- is using alcohol or drugs to cope
- has had a recent trauma

**It's okay to ask directly:** "Are you thinking about hurting yourself?" Asking does **not** put the idea in their head. It shows you care. If the answer is yes, stay with them and contact a [helpline](#7-helplines) or emergency services together.

## 5. Stress and burnout for students and developers

Exams, deadlines, job hunting, layoffs and endless screens wear people down quietly. Signs a friend (or you) may be burning out:

- always tired, even after rest
- feeling like nothing you do is good enough
- losing interest in things you used to enjoy
- getting angry or upset more easily

**What helps:** short breaks away from screens, regular sleep, talking to someone, and saying "no" to extra work when possible. If you notice these signs in a teammate or classmate, a simple "Hey, you seem stressed lately. Want to talk?" can mean a lot.

---

## 6. Take care of yourself too

Supporting someone is heavy work. You are not responsible for fixing their life.

- Take breaks when you need them.
- Talk to someone you trust about how *you* feel.
- It's okay to say: "I want to help, but I think a professional can help more than I can."
